Top Secret · Agents Only

Operation: Fourth Grade
Secret Agent Academy

Twenty-four rookie agents. Six training stations. One locked vault full of classified snacks. Your mission, should you choose to accept it: run the most talked-about class party of the year in 105 minutes flat.

The mission

The story the kids walk into

Fourth graders are the perfect age for this: old enough to crack a real cipher and take a stopwatch seriously, young enough to lose their minds over a fake mustache. The whole party hangs on one simple story.

📜 Director's briefing (read this out loud at 1:35)

"Agents, welcome to the Academy. Somewhere in this room is a vault. Inside the vault is intel so valuable it had to be locked away: the Classified Canteen. The password is scrambled and hidden across six training stations. Each station you complete earns your team one letter. Collect all six, unscramble the password, and the vault opens for everyone. Fail, and the snacks stay sealed forever. Your training starts in... three... two... one."

Then hit play on the Mission: Impossible theme. Yes, really. It works every time.

👥 Teams of 4

Six teams, each with a code name and a colored wristband (Falcon, Cobra, Shadow, Phoenix, Viper, Ghost). The teacher picks the teams ahead of time so friend groups are balanced and nobody is picked last.

🔤 Letters, not points

Every station awards one letter tile when the team finishes. The six letters spell A-G-E-N-T-S. No scoreboard, no losing team, but plenty of competition inside each station.

🎁 One shared payoff

The vault opens for everyone at once. Kids leave with a badge, a certificate, spy sunglasses, their fingerprint card, and a disguise photo.

Why this works for 9- and 10-year-olds: they crave independence and competence (real puzzles, real stopwatch), they love being "in" on a secret (cover identities, codes), they still want to move (laser maze), and they are at peak silly (disguises). Each station runs 10 minutes, which is right at the edge of their attention span, so nobody gets bored and nobody gets done too early.

Run of show

Minute by minute

Built for a 1:30–3:15 window. Shift everything if your slot is different. The rotation block is the only part that must stay rigid.

  1. 1:30

    Check-in · 5 min

    Agents enter under the caution tape. At the "Clearance Desk" (a table by the door) each kid gets an Agent ID badge on a lanyard, writes a code name, and is handed a colored wristband for their team. Theme music plays. Volunteers are already at stations.

  2. 1:35

    Director's briefing · 7 min

    Room parent (the Director) reads the briefing. Teacher is introduced as "Agent Zero, Head of the Academy." Three rules: 1) listen for the bell, 2) stay with your team, 3) agents never run indoors (they sneak). Point to each station. Teams walk to their starting station.

  3. 1:42

    Rotation block · 6 × 10 min = 60 min

    Use the rotation timer on a projector or laptop. At the bell, station leads stamp the clearance card, hand over the letter tile, and send the team clockwise. The floater keeps traffic moving. Rotation times: 1:42, 1:52, 2:02, 2:12, 2:22, 2:32.

  4. 2:42

    Final mission: Crack the Vault · 10 min

    All teams back to the rug. Teams unscramble their letters. First team to whisper the password to the Director earns the Golden Key and opens the vault for everyone. See Crack the Vault.

  5. 2:52

    Classified Canteen · 15 min

    Snacks are served by team (wristband color called one at a time to avoid a stampede). Playlist on. Floater and teacher handle allergy plates first.

  6. 3:07

    Debrief & awards · 6 min

    Director hands out superlative awards (one per team) and every agent gets a Certificate of Clearance. Group photo in sunglasses.

  7. 3:13

    Cleanup · 2 min + after

    "Agents, destroy the evidence." Kids clear their own tables. Volunteers pack bins. Room parent stays until the room looks better than it did at 1:00.

Training stations

The six stations

Each one has a 10-minute arc: 1 minute of instructions, 7 minutes of doing, 2 minutes to wrap up and stamp. Teams rotate clockwise around the room. Every station awards one letter tile.

🔦1

Laser Maze · "Infiltration Training"

Letter: APhysicalHallway corner or back of room

Red crepe-paper "lasers" zig-zag across a corner of the room at different heights. Agents go through one at a time without touching a beam while the station lead times them. Then the whole team goes through carrying a "microchip" (ping-pong ball on a spoon). Touch a laser and you restart. Best individual time and best team time go on the whiteboard.

Setup & supplies
  • 2 rolls red crepe-paper streamers, painter's tape, a stopwatch (phone), 6 plastic spoons, 6 ping-pong balls
  • Use two walls of a corner, or a row of desks on each side of an aisle, or under a long table. Tape streamers at ankle, knee, waist, and shoulder heights, criss-crossing. 12–15 beams is plenty.
  • Set up the night before if the teacher allows, otherwise arrive 45 minutes early. Keep a spare roll for repairs.
Station lead script & tips
  • "Agents, these lasers will trigger the alarm. Over, under, through, but never touch. I'll be timing you."
  • Call a "sneak zone": any kid waiting must be in spy pose (crouched). Keeps the line calm.
  • If a streamer rips, don't stop the game. Re-tape between agents.
  • Stamp the card and hand over the letter tile once every team member has crossed at least once.
🔐2

Code Breakers · "Cryptography Lab"

Letter: GBrainTable of 4

Three sealed envelopes, each a harder cipher. Envelope 1: a Caesar shift message decoded with a cardstock cipher wheel. Envelope 2: a Pigpen cipher with the key printed on the table. Envelope 3: a Morse code message tapped out on a flashlight by the station lead. Solve two of three to earn the letter; solve all three for a "Master Decoder" sticker. The messages are silly ("Agent Zero's favorite snack is pickles") so the reveal is a laugh.

Setup & supplies
  • 6 cipher wheels (two cardstock circles + a brad; make them the week before, 10 minutes each) or use the cipher tool below to print a key
  • Printed puzzle sheets (one set per team, in envelopes stamped "CLASSIFIED"), pencils, a flashlight
  • Pigpen key and Morse chart printed large and taped to the table
Station lead script & tips
  • "Someone intercepted our messages. You have 7 minutes to decode them. Work together, split the letters."
  • Hint budget: each team gets 2 free hints. Kids love rationing them.
  • Mixed-ability tip: Caesar is easy, Pigpen is medium, Morse is hard. Nudge the fast team toward Morse and the stuck team toward Caesar.
🔍3

Fingerprint Forensics · "Evidence Lab"

Letter: EScienceTable of 4 + wipes

Each agent lifts their own fingerprint: scribble a patch of pencil graphite on scrap paper, rub a fingertip in it, press onto clear tape, stick the tape onto a white index card. With a magnifying glass they classify it as a loop, whorl, or arch using the chart. Then the case file: four enlarged "suspect" prints are on the table and one "crime scene" print (who ate the Director's cookie?). The team matches the print to the suspect. Everyone takes their fingerprint card home.

Setup & supplies
  • Pencils, clear packing tape, white index cards, 4–6 magnifying glasses, wet wipes, a black tablecloth
  • Suspect cards: ask the teacher, principal, custodian, and gym teacher to let you photograph their thumbprint (or just make four distinct prints yourself). Enlarge to a half page. One of them is also the "crime scene" print.
  • Print the loop/whorl/arch chart (free from many science-education sites, or sketch it: loops curve back, whorls circle, arches hump)
Station lead script & tips
  • "Every agent has a unique print. Let's find out what yours says about you." (Nothing. But they'll want to know.)
  • Wipes before leaving the station. Graphite goes everywhere.
  • Fast finishers: lift a print from a cup or a doorknob with tape and graphite. It actually works.
🥸4

Disguise Lab · "Cover Identity"

Letter: NCreativePhoto corner

A prop bin overflows with stick-on mustaches, oversized glasses, hats, scarves, clip-on ties, and wigs. Each agent builds a cover identity and fills in their badge: cover name, hometown, cover job ("retired llama dentist"). Then a 15-second "interrogation": the station lead asks three rapid questions and the agent has to stay in character. Finish with a team photo in front of the TOP SECRET backdrop.

Setup & supplies
  • Costume props (a party-store "disguise kit" plus whatever is in your closet), hand mirror, a black plastic tablecloth taped to the wall with yellow caution tape across it
  • A phone or instant camera. Check the school photo policy; the teacher knows which kids are opted out.
  • A small whiteboard with prompt questions: "Where were you on the night of the cookie?"
Station lead script & tips
  • This is the loud station. Put it farthest from Code Breakers.
  • Shy kids: let them "interrogate" instead of being interrogated. Both earn the stamp.
  • Props go back in the bin before the bell. Make it part of the game: "Agents, remove all traces."
🧪5

Invisible Ink Lab · "Secret Messages"

Letter: TScience + craftCovered table

Agents write a secret message or draw a map with a white crayon on white paper. It's invisible. They swap with a teammate, who paints over the page with a watercolor wash and watches the message appear. Second round for fast teams: dip a cotton swab in baking-soda water, write, let dry, and reveal with purple grape juice brushed on. Take the message home in an envelope marked "BURN AFTER READING."

Setup & supplies
  • White crayons (one per kid), white printer paper, 4 cheap watercolor sets, brushes, 4 cups of water, paper towels, a plastic tablecloth, small envelopes
  • Optional round 2: baking soda, water, cotton swabs, grape juice, extra brushes
  • No heat, no lemon juice, no candles. Nothing here needs a flame, which keeps the teacher happy.
Station lead script & tips
  • "Real agents never write anything they can't make disappear. Press hard with the crayon."
  • Water cups tip. Fill them only a third full.
  • Have a drying spot (a clothesline with clips or a spare desk) so messages don't smear in the envelope.
🎯6

Field Agent Skills · "Steady Hands"

Letter: SMinute-to-win-itTwo desks + floor

Three 60-second challenges, each agent tries each one. Microchip Transfer: move 10 pom-poms from one bowl to another using chopsticks. Defuse the Device: stack 10 cups into a pyramid and back down to a single stack. Getaway Balloon: blow a balloon across a taped course on the floor using only a straw. The station lead keeps a leaderboard, and the team's letter is earned when every agent has completed any two challenges.

Setup & supplies
  • Chopsticks (or kid tongs), 40 pom-poms, 4 bowls, 20 plastic cups, 6 balloons, 6 straws, painter's tape for the floor course, a phone timer
  • Pom-poms instead of candy on purpose: no allergy issues, no "did you eat your microchips."
Station lead script & tips
  • "Field agents need steady hands under pressure. 60 seconds. Go."
  • Run two challenges at once with two kids each so nobody is watching for long.
  • Whiteboard "Academy Records" for each challenge. Kids will ask to retry. Let them if there's time.

Rotation map: 1 Laser Maze → 2 Code Breakers → 3 Fingerprints → 4 Disguise → 5 Invisible Ink → 6 Field Skills → back to 1. Put the quiet stations (2, 3, 5) on one side of the room and the loud ones (1, 4, 6) on the other. Each team starts at a different station, so every station always has exactly one team.

Final mission

Crack the Vault

The payoff. Every team now holds six letter tiles. The Director calls everyone to the rug.

  1. Unscramble. "Agents, the password is hidden in your tiles. Teams, you have 90 seconds." The letters A G E N T S spell AGENTS. Most teams get it fast. That's fine. Speed is the competition.
  2. Whisper it. First team to whisper the correct password to the Director wins the Golden Key (a large gold cardboard key). Second and third get "Deputy Key" stickers. Nobody is shamed.
  3. The twist. The vault has a second lock. Agent Zero (the teacher) is the only one with the code, and Agent Zero has been "captured." The class must complete one group challenge to free them: 30 seconds of total silence (brutal and hilarious), or a whole-class wave, or everyone doing the secret handshake. Teacher's pick.
  4. Open the vault. The Golden Key team breaks the paper seals and opens the box. Inside: a sign reading "CLASSIFIED CANTEEN: NOW OPEN," 24 pairs of spy sunglasses, and the "Golden Tickets" each team needs to get served first.

🗄️ Building the vault

  • A large cardboard box (a moving box or copy-paper box) wrapped in black paper or painted gray
  • A paper-plate "dial" with numbers drawn on it, brad in the center so it spins
  • Six paper "SECURITY SEAL" strips across the lid, one per station letter, that get torn when the vault opens
  • Optional upgrade: a real 4-digit combination lock on a hasp or a lockable cash box. Use digits from stations 1–4 instead of letters for a true escape-room feel.
  • Hide it in plain sight under a black tablecloth all party long so kids keep eyeing it
Snacks

The Classified Canteen

Every item is nut-free, mostly store-bought with ingredient labels (many schools require this), and gets a spy name on a tented card. Kids are served by team so the line stays short.

🍪 Fingerprint Cookies

Thumbprint jam cookies. Ties to station 3. Bakery or store-bought in a sealed package with a label.

🍩 Decoder Donuts

Mini powdered donuts, each with a toothpick flag bearing a letter. Spell your name on your plate.

🍓 Surveillance Skewers

Fruit kebabs (grape, strawberry, melon, grape) that look like antennas. Prep that morning, keep cold.

🥕 Camouflage Cups

Hummus or ranch at the bottom of a clear cup, veggie sticks standing up. Veggies, disguised as fun.

🥨 Top Secret Trail Mix

Build-your-own bar: pretzels, cereal, mini marshmallows, chocolate chips, raisins, popcorn. Each kid fills a bag stamped CLASSIFIED. Nut-free by design.

💧 Agent H2O & Decoy Lemonade

Water bottles with a printed "AGENT H2O" label wrapped on, plus a pitcher of lemonade. No red drinks near the carpet.

Allergy protocol: 1) Get the class allergy list from the teacher three weeks out and re-confirm the week of. 2) Nut-free everything; check labels for "may contain." 3) Prep a separate, clearly labeled safe plate for any child with dairy, egg, or gluten needs and hand it to them first. 4) Keep every package label on the table so parents and the teacher can read them. 5) Ask the teacher whether home-baked items are allowed at your school. Many districts say no.

Volunteer snack sign-up keeps your cost down. Ask for: 2 packs of cookies, 2 boxes of mini donuts, 3 fruit platters or 48 skewers, 2 veggie trays with dips, trail mix ingredients (6 slots), 24 water bottles, 2 lemonade jugs, 60 plates / napkins / cups, 30 snack bags. The sign-up template is on the Sign-Ups page.

Atmosphere

Set the scene

🎨 Decor that costs almost nothing

  • The door: yellow caution tape in an X with a sign, "RESTRICTED AREA: AGENTS ONLY."
  • Colors: black, red, and yellow balloons in clusters of three. Skip helium.
  • Mission Control: the projector shows the rotation timer, or a looping "SYSTEM ONLINE" screen.
  • Wanted board: goofy "MOST WANTED" posters of the teacher, principal, and custodian (ask permission, and let them pick the photo).
  • Table tents: each station has a manila folder standing up, stamped CLASSIFIED, with the station name inside.
  • Dossier invitations: a week before, each kid gets a manila envelope with a "YOUR MISSION" letter and a blank Agent ID to fill out at home.
  • Fingerprint wall: a big sheet of butcher paper where every agent adds a thumbprint in washable ink on the way in.

👔 Dress code

Kids: "wear black, or dress like a spy." Volunteers: sunglasses and a dark shirt. The Director wears a trench coat or blazer and carries a clipboard. Agent Zero (teacher) gets a fedora.

Debrief

Awards that make everyone a winner

Every agent gets a Certificate of Clearance and a badge sticker. Then one superlative per team, decided by the station leads during the canteen.

🧠 Master Decoder

Cracked all three ciphers or carried the team through Code Breakers.

👻 Stealthiest Agent

Cleanest laser-maze run. Zero beams touched.

🤲 Steadiest Hands

Academy record at the microchip transfer or cup stack.

🥸 Deepest Cover

Best disguise and never broke character in interrogation.

🔬 Chief Forensic Scientist

Matched the suspect print first, or helped the whole team classify.

🤝 Best Teammate

The kid who made sure everyone got a turn. The teacher usually knows exactly who this is.

Debrief script: "Agents, the Academy has reviewed your files. Every one of you has earned full clearance. Stand up if you went through the laser maze. Stand if you cracked a code. Stand if you have a fingerprint card in your pocket. Look around. That's a room full of fully trained agents. Sunglasses on. Group photo. Three... two... one... classified!"

📋 Station cards

Copy each one into a document, print one per station lead, and tape it inside their manila folder. Everything a volunteer needs on one page.

STATION 1 — LASER MAZE ("Infiltration Training")   Letter tile: A
Time per team: 10 min (1 min intro / 7 min play / 2 min stamp & reset)

SAY: "Agents, these lasers trigger the alarm. Over, under, through — never touch. I'm timing you."

RUN IT:
1. One agent at a time through the maze. Stopwatch each run. Touch a beam = restart.
2. Waiting agents hold "spy pose" (crouched, quiet).
3. Round 2: team crosses carrying a ping-pong ball on a spoon. Team time on the whiteboard.
4. At the bell: stamp the clearance card, hand over letter A, send team to Station 2.

FIXES: Torn streamer? Re-tape between runs, don't stop play. Kid afraid? Let them pick their own path or go with a buddy.
BIN: 2 rolls red streamers, painter's tape, 6 spoons, 6 ping-pong balls, stopwatch, stamp, 6 letter-A tiles.
STATION 2 — CODE BREAKERS ("Cryptography Lab")   Letter tile: G
Time per team: 10 min

SAY: "Someone intercepted our messages. You have 7 minutes to decode them. Split up the letters and work together."

RUN IT:
1. Envelope 1 (easy): Caesar shift message — use the cipher wheel, shift printed on the envelope.
2. Envelope 2 (medium): Pigpen cipher — key is taped to the table.
3. Envelope 3 (hard): Morse — flash it with the flashlight, slowly, twice. Chart on the table.
4. Two solved = letter G. Three solved = Master Decoder sticker for each agent.
5. Hints: each team gets 2. Make them ask for them.

FIXES: Stuck team → start with Caesar, give the first letter free. Speedy team → Morse, then have them WRITE a message for the next team.
BIN: 6 cipher wheels, 6 sets of puzzle envelopes, pencils, flashlight, Pigpen key, Morse chart, stamp, 6 letter-G tiles, Master Decoder stickers.
STATION 3 — FINGERPRINT FORENSICS ("Evidence Lab")   Letter tile: E
Time per team: 10 min

SAY: "Every agent on earth has a unique print. Let's lift yours and find out what kind it is."

RUN IT:
1. Scribble a dark graphite patch on scrap paper. Rub fingertip in it.
2. Press a piece of clear tape on the fingertip, peel, stick to a white index card. Write name + date.
3. Magnifying glass: loop, whorl, or arch? Use the chart. Write it on the card.
4. CASE FILE: "Who ate the Director's cookie?" Match the crime-scene print to one of 4 suspects.
5. Wipes before leaving. At the bell: stamp, hand over letter E, send to Station 4.

FIXES: Smudgy print? Less graphite, lighter press. Fast team → lift a print off a cup.
BIN: pencils, scrap paper, clear packing tape, index cards, 6 magnifying glasses, print-type chart, 4 suspect cards + crime-scene card, wet wipes, black tablecloth, stamp, 6 letter-E tiles.
STATION 4 — DISGUISE LAB ("Cover Identity")   Letter tile: N
Time per team: 10 min

SAY: "A good agent can become anyone. Build your cover identity. Then survive interrogation."

RUN IT:
1. Each agent grabs props and builds a disguise (2 min).
2. On the badge: cover name, hometown, cover job. Sillier is better.
3. Interrogation: 3 rapid questions ("Where were you last night?" "What's your job?" "Favorite food?"). Stay in character for 15 seconds = pass.
4. Team photo at the TOP SECRET backdrop. CHECK the photo opt-out list first (teacher has it).
5. Props back in the bin: "Agents, remove all traces." Stamp, letter N, send to Station 5.

FIXES: Shy agent → they become the interrogator. Props running low → hats and sunglasses count.
BIN: prop kit, hand mirror, backdrop (already on wall), phone/camera, question card, markers, stamp, 6 letter-N tiles.
STATION 5 — INVISIBLE INK LAB ("Secret Messages")   Letter tile: T
Time per team: 10 min

SAY: "Real agents never write anything they can't make disappear. Press HARD with the crayon."

RUN IT:
1. White crayon on white paper: write a message or draw a map for a teammate. Press hard.
2. Swap papers. Paint a watercolor wash over the whole page. Message appears.
3. Round 2 (if time): cotton swab + baking-soda water to write, let dry, reveal with grape juice.
4. Messages go in an envelope marked BURN AFTER READING. Dry them on the clothesline first.
5. At the bell: stamp, letter T, send to Station 6.

FIXES: Can't see message? Crayon too light — press harder. Water cup spill → paper towels are in the bin, keep cups 1/3 full.
BIN: 24 white crayons, paper, 4 watercolor sets, brushes, 4 cups, paper towels, tablecloth, 30 envelopes, baking soda, grape juice, swabs, clothesline + clips, stamp, 6 letter-T tiles.
STATION 6 — FIELD AGENT SKILLS ("Steady Hands")   Letter tile: S
Time per team: 10 min

SAY: "Field agents need steady hands under pressure. Sixty seconds. Go."

RUN IT (run two challenges side by side, 2 agents each):
A. MICROCHIP TRANSFER — move 10 pom-poms bowl to bowl with chopsticks. Time it.
B. DEFUSE THE DEVICE — stack 10 cups into a pyramid and back to one stack. Time it.
C. GETAWAY BALLOON — blow a balloon along the taped floor course with a straw. Time it.
Each agent completes any 2 of 3 = letter S for the team. Keep an "Academy Records" leaderboard on the whiteboard.

FIXES: Cup tower keeps falling → stack on the floor. Balloon drifts → shorten the course.
BIN: 40 pom-poms, 4 bowls, chopsticks/tongs, 20 cups, 6 balloons, 6 straws, tape (course is pre-taped), timer, whiteboard marker, stamp, 6 letter-S tiles.

Contingencies

Backup plans

🙋 Only 4 volunteers showed up

Merge to 4 stations: Laser Maze, Code Breakers, Fingerprints + Invisible Ink (same table, two halves), Disguise + Field Skills. Run 4 rotations of 13 minutes. The teacher floats. The Director runs check-in and then the vault. Hand out two letter tiles per station (A+G, E+N, T+S, plus a blank "wild" tile) so the password still spells AGENTS.

🏫 Tiny classroom

Laser maze goes under a long table or in the hallway if the teacher okays it. Field Skills moves to the rug. Disguise photo backdrop goes on the back of the door. Everything else is a desk cluster.

🚫 No food allowed

The vault still works. Fill it with the sunglasses, glow sticks, mini notebooks ("agent logbooks"), and spy pens. The canteen slot becomes a 15-minute bonus round: whole-class "Spy Says" (Simon Says) and a freeze-dance to the Pink Panther theme.

Skip lanyards (tape badges to shirts), sunglasses (certificates are the prize), and the prop kit (kids bring a disguise from home). Streamers, tape, cardstock, crayons, watercolors, pom-poms, cups, balloons, and a stamp come to about $60. Snacks via sign-up.

⏳ 20 minutes left and they're done

Back-pocket games: Spy Says; Secret Handshake (teams invent one, then teach it to another team); Agent Down (freeze dance, last one standing is the mole); Who's the Mole? (teacher secretly taps one kid, class asks yes/no questions to find them).

🧒 A kid is having a hard time

Give them a job: "Agent, I need you on timer duty with me." Or make them the Director's assistant who announces rotations. A role beats a time-out every time.

Rest of the year

Other party themes that work for 4th grade

Same bones (stations, rotation timer, one shared payoff), different costume. The Halloween party is fully built out; the rest are sketches on the events hub.

🎃 Fall Fest Carnival

Pumpkin bowling, donut-on-a-string, "guess the gourd" weight, candy-corn relay, cider and popcorn. Payoff: a pumpkin-decorating contest judged by the principal.

❄️ Winter Science Lab

Instant "snow" (sodium polyacrylate), borax crystal snowflakes, ice-cube fishing with string and salt, cocoa bar. Payoff: a snowball fight with crumpled paper, timed.

💌 Kindness Carnival

Valentine's without the pressure: compliment-card writing, "kindness bingo," a friendship-bracelet station, a class mural. Payoff: every kid gets a bag of cards from everyone.

🧪 Mad Scientist Academy

Elephant toothpaste demo, oobleck, balloon rockets on a string, film-canister poppers (outside). Payoff: a class "lab coat" photo and a periodic-table snack board.

🏆 End-of-Year Olympics

Outdoor field day: sponge relay, hula-hoop pass, three-legged race, water-balloon toss, a "torch" relay. Payoff: medals and popsicles, and the teacher gets pied (with whipped cream, with permission).

🧩 Classroom Escape Room

The spy party's sequel: four locked boxes, each puzzle unlocks the next, whole class works together against the clock. Payoff: the last box holds the end-of-year class slideshow.
