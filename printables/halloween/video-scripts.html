Self-recording guide

Reality-TV confessional style: you sit and talk to the camera, deadpan. No interviewer, so prop up your own phone, or let whoever sent you this hold it, and record it in five minutes whenever you have a free moment.

Setup

  • Nobody films you. Prop your phone horizontal against a stack of books or a mug, at face height, about an arm's length away. Sit in a chair against a plain wall or a bookshelf.
  • Light: face a window or a lamp. Don't sit with a window behind you.
  • Eyeline: look just to the side of the lens, as if a producer is sitting next to the phone. Glancing at the lens once or twice is fine.
  • Tone: deadpan beats hammy. You are a sincere person who has nothing to hide and is slightly annoyed to be asked.

Recording

  • The script: read it from a second screen or a printout taped just under the phone. The (beat) and (pause) marks are where the laughs live. Hold them longer than feels natural.
  • Takes: two max. Send the slightly flubbed one. Don't edit anything.
  • Length: 60 to 90 seconds. Start recording, wait two seconds, begin. Stop two seconds after the last line.
  • Send it: text or AirDrop the raw video to whoever sent you the script. They add the title card and load it onto the classroom laptop.

Making the script yours

Words in [brackets] are adjustable. The facts the kids check (shoe size, where you were, what you wore, how you feel about squishies) live on your Suspect Statement form, not in this video; your answer card tells you what to write there. The box at the top of your script lists the one or two things the video has to agree with, and the lines you should keep word for word. Everything else is flavor. Swap in your real life, your real subject, your real habits. Wear whatever you like. True details get the biggest laughs.

The ask (email or text to each suspect)

Subject: 5 minutes of acting for the 4th grade Halloween party? Hi [Name], Mrs. Driscoll's class Halloween party on Oct 30 is a whodunit, and the kids are going to investigate four staff "suspects." Would you be one of the three who record a statement? All it takes is a 90-second video of yourself that you can record alone on your phone whenever you have a free minute. No one films you, no one edits you, and you don't need to attend the party. Attached are your script and a one-page Suspect Statement form with an answer card. Four answers on the form are scripted so the kids' clues line up; the rest is yours, and true is funnier. The video is a monologue, so just talk to the camera. Quick tips: • Phone horizontal, propped at face height, plain wall behind you • Look just beside the lens, like a producer is sitting there • Deadpan. Sincere. Slightly annoyed to be asked. • Hold the pauses. Two takes max. Send me the raw video and the filled-in form by [Oct 23]. The kids will watch it on the projector during the party and, I promise, lose their minds. Thank you! [Your name] · [room parent for Mrs. Driscoll's class] · [phone]

The Custodian gets a different ask: "You're a suspect who declined to comment. That's it. That's the whole job. Please act shifty in the hallway on Oct 30."

TITLE CARD · INTERVIEW 1 · THE PRINCIPAL · 9:12 AM

MAKE IT YOURS (read before you record)

  • Your facts (shoe size, what you wore, and so on) live on your Suspect Statement form; the answer card tells you what to write there. On camera only two things have to agree with the form: you were "at a meeting" at [2:15], and you own exactly one squishy, [a cat]. Want a different squishy? Change it on the form too, and tell the room parent.
  • Must match the real jar: "the big one, on the high shelf [by the window], with [three] strips of tape." Describe the actual jar in the actual spot.
  • Free to change: [four minutes], the kind of meeting, [tie or collar], what the squishy is for. Add one true detail about yourself; it lands harder.
  • Keep exactly: saying "squishies" by accident, then "candy," then trying to un-say it. That slip is the whole party.

Words in [brackets] are yours to adjust. Say them naturally, without the brackets.

(Sit. Look just beside the camera. Check your watch.)

I have a meeting in [four minutes], so let's make this quick.

You want to know where I was yesterday at [2:15]. I was in a meeting. I'm always in a meeting. Yesterday it was a meeting about the next meeting.

And you want to know what I know about the missing candy. Let me be very clear about this. I have not heard ANYTHING, and I know NOTHING, about anyone stealing the squishies.

(beat)

I mean candy.

(beat)

...Did I say squishies? What was I thinking! Candy. The candy. I know nothing about the candy.

(pause. Straighten your [tie or collar].)

I did not say squishies. I may have said squishies. It's a very common word. People say it all the time. In meetings. I am the principal of this school. I say a lot of words every day. Statistically, some of them are going to be "squishies."

(too fast)

And for the record, I have never even seen that jar. The big one. On the high shelf [by the window]. With the [three] strips of tape on the lid and the label that says "do not open."

(realize what you just said)

...Which I can only describe because people have described it to me. In meetings.

(pause)

Do I own any squishies myself? I'm the principal.

(beat)

...One. It's [a cat]. It's on my desk. It's for stress. This is stressful. (look at watch) I'm late.

(Stand up and walk out of frame. Leave the phone recording for 3 more seconds, then say, from off camera, slightly muffled:)

Is it off? Did it get the squishy thing? ...Can we cut the squishy thing?

(Then stop recording.)

Ties the clues to the suspects

Suspect statement forms

A one-page questionnaire each suspect fills in by hand. Four of its answers are the four traits the clues test, and we script those; everything else is theirs. The kids compare each clue to the forms, so no real person has to wear, own, or be anything.

Who fills one out

  • The Principal, the Gym Teacher, the Librarian: send each their script, their answer card, and a blank form together. Forms come back with the videos by [Oct 23].
  • The Custodian: nothing to do. The form is pre-stamped DECLINED TO COMMENT with the Bureau's "records" printed on it.
  • The Chief (teacher): fills one out too, from her answer card. It matches every clue. It stays in the Detective's folder until deliberation.

Where they go on the day

  • Four forms (not the Chief's) go on the suspect board beside the WANTED posters, from 1:30. Squads can walk over and read them any time.
  • The typed summary of the same four forms is the suspect table in every case file, so squads can cross off at their station without leaving it.
  • At 2:28, when a squad has crossed off all four, the Detective produces the fifth form. See below.

The deliberation script (2:28)

A squad says: "Everyone's crossed off." The Detective, loud enough for the room:

"Then your list is too short. Four clues, four facts about the thief. Size 10. In the building at 2:15. Orange sweater. Loves squishies. Who else was in this building yesterday?" (Let them guess. Someone will say the Chief. The Chief: "I am NOT a suspect.")

"Chief, you filled out a statement form like everyone else. For completeness. I have it right here." (Pin it to the board next to the other four. Read it aloud, including the crossed-out "Don't be ridiculous.") "Detectives, check it against your clues. Then write your warrants."

Optional tell for the sharp ones: the Chief wears something orange all party (a scarf from the bin is fine), and keeps one squishy on her desk, "confiscated."
